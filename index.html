01 — Input stack

Six bands, one pass

The network never sees a photograph. It sees radar backscatter, before and after the flood, plus terrain — six co‑registered channels read together at 256×256 pixels per patch.

01
VV_post
Post‑flood VV backscatter — 1–20 Sep 2024 mean composite. Open water reflects radar away from the sensor, so flooded ground reads darker.
Sentinel‑1
02
VH_post
Post‑flood cross‑pol backscatter — catches double‑bounce returns from flooded vegetation and structures that VV alone misses.
Sentinel‑1
03
VV_pre / VH_pre
Pre‑flood baseline — 1–20 Aug 2024. Gives the network something dry to compare against.
Sentinel‑1
04
log_ratio
Change‑detection layer — post minus pre, in decibels. Flooded pixels show a sharp negative excursion here.
Derived
05
elevation
SRTM terrain — keeps the model from chasing radar shadow and layover in hill terrain at the basin's edge.

Model summary — Download result
  1. Assemble a six‑band GeoTIFF — VV_post, VH_post, VV_pre, VH_pre, log_ratio, elevation, in that order, co‑registered at a common grid.
  2. Drop it in — anything larger than 256×256 is cropped; anything smaller is padded.
  3. Read the mask — a binary flood/no‑flood image, plus a summary percentage of the patch covered.
What this is, and isn't. This is a research demonstrator trained and evaluated on a single event over the Kosi basin. It is not an operational flood‑warning system, and hasn't been validated against independently surveyed ground truth. Treat its output as a research signal, not a dispatch order.
